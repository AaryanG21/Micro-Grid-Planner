02 / DESCENT

Site R-07.

11.24 N, 76.89 E. Ridge valley, no grid connection within 34 km, 128 households detected from building footprints.

03 / SOLAR

The sun does most of the work.

Modules face the sun path at 13 degrees tilt. Plane-of-array irradiance follows the cosine of incidence, so array output tracks the light rather than the clock.

04 / HYDRO

The river works the night shift.

48 m of head, 1.6 cubic metres per second. Run-of-river hydro is the reason this site does not need a diesel genset after dark.

05 / WIND

The ridge fills the gaps.

Three 27 m rotors at 7.5 m/s mean wind. Wind and solar are anti-correlated here, which is what lets the battery stay small.

06 / DELIVERY

128 roofs, lit.

Storage carries the evening peak. What the planner actually outputs is this: a bill of materials, a cost, and a date the thing pays for itself.

What is actually being computed

Solar uses incidence = |90 - (elevation + tilt)| and plane irradiance = 1000 cos(incidence) x 0.86. Hydro uses P = 9.81 Q H x 0.85. Wind uses P = 0.5 rho A v cubed x Cp with Cp 0.42.

The globe is drawn from coarse lat/lon coastlines projected orthographically. Polygons crossing the horizon are clipped against the z = 0 great circle with interpolated limb vertices, which is what stops a continent smearing across the whole disc when it rotates out of view.
